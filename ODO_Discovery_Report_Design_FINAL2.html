SWOT — where the business stands

A SWOT is a simple way to look at a business from four angles at once: Strengths and Weaknesses are about the business today — what’s already working, and what isn’t. Opportunities and Threats are about what’s ahead — what could be gained, and what could go wrong if nothing changes. Every point below is drawn from the findings in this report, not a generic template.

STRENGTHS2

  • 6 of 6 recommended security headers are in place. [E6]
  • Operations are already largely automated (stated). [A14]

WEAKNESSES5

  • No SPF record is published for the domain. [E1]
  • No DMARC record is published. [E2]
  • No privacy policy was found on the website. [E7]
  • Nobody is specifically responsible for IT or security. [A4]
  • MFA is not required on email or key business systems. [A5]

OPPORTUNITIES5

  • Mail Shield: address the missing SPF and DMARC records and verify DKIM signing. [E1, E2, E3]
  • Security Rules Setup: establish vulnerability-reporting and incident-response guidance. [E8, A7]
  • Security Response: review recovery readiness and response planning. [A6, A7]
  • Cyber Health Check: clarify responsibility for IT and security. [A3, A4]
  • Compliance Check: review the missing public privacy policy. [E7]

THREATS4

  • Email impersonation exposure associated with the missing SPF record. [E1]
  • Domain spoofing exposure associated with the missing DMARC record. [E2]
  • Privacy concerns associated with the absence of a visible policy. [E7]
  • Delayed or unclear security decisions where no responsible owner is assigned. [A4]

Read the evidence alongside the summary. Each reference points back to a finding, its source and its checking date. An unconfirmed observation remains something to verify before deciding on an action.
